# ingest/

The ingest script runs on a laptop (Node 22.18 or newer, no dependencies). Each run collects public filings, releases and economic numbers, turns them into short cards, checks every card, and writes it to Supabase.

## What one run does

1. **Macro numbers** (`src/sources/macro.ts`): fetches FRED observations for 18 series (the 12 listed here plus six finance series, see the end of this item): DGS2, DGS10, DFF, CPIAUCSL, UNRATE, DCOILWTICO (daily or monthly), GASREGW and ICSA (weekly), RSAFS, HOUST and PAYEMS (monthly) and A191RL1Q225SBEA (real GDP growth, quarterly). It formats them ("4.02%", "+6 bp" for rates and "+0.1 pts" for unemployment, GDP and CPI, "$4.33", "199,000", "$745.0B", CPI as a year-over-year rate) and stores them with `api.upsert_macro`. A **data card** is published only for a new observation that moved enough: the fed funds rate by 0.05 points or more, oil by 2%, gas by 1%, jobless claims by 5%. Monthly and quarterly series get one card per new period. Weekly cards say "week of" / "week ending" and "prior week"; quarterly cards say "Q2 2026" and "prior quarter". Data cards are templates (no AI) and cite the original agency. MORTGAGE30US (Freddie Mac copyright) is on a forbidden list and is refused. The numbers panel in the app shows a top set of 8 (column `in_numbers` in `core.macro_series`). **Finance and rates (2026-11-08):** six more Federal Reserve Board series (DGS3MO, DGS5, DGS30, EFFR, DFEDTARU, DFEDTARL; numbers panel only for the yields (the daily yield-curve card covers them), EFFR and the lower limit; a card at 0.05 points for the target-range upper limit) and a computed 10-year minus 2-year yield gap (`SPREAD_10Y2Y`, from DGS10 and DGS2 in the same run; FRED's own T10Y2Y and SOFR are not used because their pages carry a copyright flag). None of these is in the app's numbers panel. **M9 data-card templates (workstream 9):** (a) rate moves read in **basis points** (`+5 bp`, series in `BP_SERIES`); the card says "up 5 basis points"; unemployment, GDP and CPI keep percentage points. (b) Every FRED/Treasury data card is category **Economy** (oil and gas stay Energy), so "Hide Business topics" no longer hides them. (c) The detail key points no longer repeat the number: value with its date, previous reading (rates and percentages), Fed target range on the fed funds card, how often it is published, the agency. (d) Each series has a hand-written **why-it-matters** line (`WHY` in `macro.ts`: a definition or a fact about the agency's data, no AI, no prediction, no advice, never repeating the series definition); it ends the card body and sits in the detail sheet. Template cards keep ai_generated false (the app shows "No AI used"). (e) **One daily Treasury yield-curve card** (`buildYieldCurveCard`, dedupe key `YIELDCURVE:<date>`, series id `YIELDCURVE`): 2, 5, 10 and 30-year plus the 10-year minus 2-year gap, moves in basis points, headline "Treasury yields rise|fall|mixed|unchanged, 10-year at 4.86%", source the Federal Reserve Board H.15 release shared through FRED. The per-maturity cards (DGS2, DGS3MO, DGS5, DGS10, DGS30) are numbers-panel only (`dataCardMinMove` 99). The card is built from the points already fetched for the Numbers panel and is skipped (retried next run) unless all four maturities have a reading on the same date as the 10-year, so the card and the ticker show the same value and date. `config.yieldCurve.minMovePoints` 0 = a card on every new reading (about 250 a year, one live at a time because the supersede trigger hides older ones). (f) The fed funds card is labelled **effective** ("Fed funds (effective)", "Effective fed funds rate") and states the target range (from DFEDTARL/DFEDTARU, fetched first in the same run) as a key point. (g) Agency template cards (Fed, BEA, SEC press, ...) no longer show a "From / Published / Title" dump: key points are plain sentences (who published and when, what the agency is). (h) FAA airworthiness directives from the Federal Register are filed under Recalls & Safety at publish. Template version `tpl-v6`. Before using them in production check each series' notes on its FRED page for a "Copyright" line.
1a. **Treasury FiscalData** (`src/sources/fiscaldata.ts`, source `treasury`): two JSON calls to `api.fiscaldata.treasury.gov` (no key): average interest rates on marketable Bills, Notes and Bonds (monthly) and total national debt (daily). Values arrive as strings and "null"; both are handled. Numbers go through the same path as FRED series (`api.upsert_macro`); only the Notes rate becomes a data card (`config.fiscalData.cardSeries`), the debt is numbers only. Cited as "U.S. Department of the Treasury, Fiscal Service"; the fact links to the agency page, never FRED. Run daily.
1b. **Fed speeches and testimony** (`src/sources/fedspeech.ts`, source `fedspeech`): `federalreserve.gov/feeds/speeches.xml` (page host). Board members only. Feed title and description go to the AI (no page download); items dated in the future (embargo) wait for their pubDate; 30-day window. Public domain, 25-word copy limit, credit "Source: Board of Governors of the Federal Reserve System". No template cards (AI relevance only).
1c. **SEC press releases** (`src/sources/secpress.ts`, source `secpress`): `sec.gov/news/pressreleases.rss` (page host), feed text only, 14-day window, category markets. The feed page states no reuse terms, so the stricter 10-word copy limit applies (license class `company_filing`). Credit "Source: U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission". No template cards.
1d. **FOMC statements and minutes** need no new source: they arrive in `press_monetary.xml`, already read by the `fed` source. They now carry the doc label "FOMC statement" / "FOMC minutes" and the Board credit line (event type `fed`, 25-word limit). The NY Fed (SOFR, repo) is deliberately not used (R-37).
2. **Federal Reserve** (`src/sources/fed.ts`): reads the press-release RSS feeds (monetary policy + all releases), drops anything older than 7 days, and fetches each release page from `www.federalreserve.gov`.
3. **BEA** (`src/sources/bea.ts`): reads the U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis news-release RSS from `apps.bea.gov` (an API host, not a page host) and keeps releases from the last 30 days. Only the feed's own title and description are summarized; BEA pages are never downloaded. Cards are public domain, category `economy`, event type `macro_release`, and carry "Source: U.S. Bureau of Economic Analysis".
3a. **BLS data API** (`src/sources/bls.ts`, `src/agencyRun.ts`, source `bls`): public domain. One GET per series to `api.bls.gov/publicAPI/v1/timeseries/data/<id>` (v1, no key: 25 requests a day; with `BLS_API_KEY` set it uses v2, 500 a day). Series (`config.bls.series`): average hourly earnings `CES0500000003` (shown as the yearly change), Employment Cost Index 12-month change `CIU1010000000000A` (quarterly), JOLTS job openings `JTS000000000000000JOL`. Annual averages (M13, Q05) are dropped. Same Numbers/data-card path as the FRED series (`publishSeries` in `macroRun.ts`): one data card per new period, dedupe key `<series>:<period date>`, so a rerun adds nothing. Cited as "U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics", linked to the agency page, never FRED. Cron: 14:12 and 20:12 UTC (3 requests per run, 6 a day). Expected cards: about 3 a month (monthly series on release day, ECI quarterly). No AI, no page text.
3b. **BEA data API** (`src/sources/beadata.ts`, `src/agencyRun.ts`, source `beadata`): one GET per NIPA table to `apps.bea.gov/api/data/` with `BEA_API_KEY` (env only; limits 100 requests, 100 MB and 30 errors per minute): T20804 PCE price index (PCE and core PCE, shown as the yearly change), T20600 personal income, T10105 GDP in dollars (`config.beaData.tables`). Real GDP growth stays on the FRED series `A191RL1Q225SBEA`. **OFF by default** (`config.beaData.enabled = false`, or secret `BEA_DATA_ENABLED=1`): the BEA API terms need a sign-in to read and no public-domain statement was found on the pages that could be read (`docs/work/sources-bls-bea.md`). Same data-card path and dedupe as BLS. Cron: 14:42 and 20:42 UTC (3 requests per run). Expected cards: about 4 a month once on.
3c. **More FRED series** (`src/sources/moreSeries.ts`, same path as item 1, source `macro`): 16 more series, all US federal statistics whose FRED notes carry no Copyright line (checked 2026-11-11): CCSA continuing claims (weekly), CIVPART, U6RATE, CPILFESL, PPIFIS, PCEPI, PCEPILFE, PSAVERT, INDPRO, TCU, TOTALSL, WALCL (weekly), M2SL, PERMIT, HSN1F, DGORDER. Cards cite the agency by name plus "via FRED". Weekly thresholds in `config.dataCardMinMove` (CCSA 3 percent, WALCL 0.2 percent); monthly series get one card per period. A year-over-year series with a missing year-ago month (CPILFESL for October 2026, the 2025 shutdown gap) produces no card until the data exists. Migration `20261134000000_m9c_series.sql` seeds the `core.macro_series` rows. Expected cards: about 20 a month.
3d. **BLS release calendar** (`src/sources/blscal.ts`, `src/agencyRunData.ts`, source `blscal`): one GET of the BLS ICS schedule (`www.bls.gov/schedule/news_release/bls.ics`, page host, public domain). Keeps eight major reports (jobs, CPI, PPI, JOLTS, ECI, productivity, import prices, real earnings; whole-name match, regional rows are ignored) and makes one template card per release dated within the next `config.agency.blscal.leadDays` (3) Eastern days, cap 3 new cards a run. Key `BLSCAL_<REPORT>:<release date>`, so a newer notice replaces the older one. Cron daily 21:20 UTC. About 8 to 10 cards a month.
3e. **Treasury Monthly Treasury Statement** (`src/sources/mts.ts`, source `mts`): one FiscalData call (`mts_table_1`, no key) for the newest month: receipts, outlays, deficit or surplus, the same month a year earlier and the fiscal year to date. Positive `current_month_dfct_sur_amt` is a deficit. Key `MTS_BUDGET:<record date>`. Cron weekdays 19:36 UTC. One card a month.
3f. **EIA refinery supply** (`src/sources/eia.ts` `supplyCard`, inside source `eia`): refinery utilization headline with crude production, crude imports and total product supplied in the detail (routes `/petroleum/sum/sndw/data/` and `/petroleum/pnp/wiup/data/`, 2 requests). Key `EIA_SUPPLY:<week>`. About 4 cards a month. All agency template cards use series-style keys (`EIA_PETROLEUM`, `EIA_GAS_STORAGE`, `AUCTION_10Y_NOTE`, `CENSUS_RETAIL`) because `core.supersede_series` keeps one live card per key prefix.
    Live field check (read-only, run by hand): `ENV_FILE=<path to .env> node --experimental-strip-types ingest/scripts/live-m9c-check.ts [--save <fixture dir>]`.
4. **Federal Register** (`src/sources/fedreg.ts`): one JSON query to the Federal Register documents API (no key): final rules from agencies whose rules reach people's wallets (CFPB, Transportation, FTC, IRS, Treasury, the Fed, SEC, FCC, Energy, Education). The "significant rules from any agency" query is off (`config.fedRegister.significantQuery = false`). Federal Register cards are only published when the AI scores them 3 or more for everyday relevance; there are no template Federal Register cards. Results are merged on `document_number` and kept for 14 days. The AI sees only each document's `abstract`; a document without one (corrections, for example) is skipped and logged. No page is downloaded (`www.federalregister.gov` is on the API host list only). The card headline is written by the AI (or, for a template card, is the title cut to 90 characters); the source link is the document's `html_url`; future-dated (scheduled) issues get today's time; category `policy`, event type empty (ranking default). Credit line: "Source: Federal Register (National Archives), <agency>". Copy limit 25 words. Poll at most hourly (no published rate limit). **FAA airworthiness directives** (title contains "Airworthiness Directive(s)", `isAirworthinessDirective`) are near-identical aircraft fix orders: at most `config.fedRegister.maxAirworthinessPerRun` (1) **new** one becomes a card per run (newest first; a directive an earlier run already handled does not use up the cap); the others are skipped and logged `skip faa_directive_cap`. `src/ranking.ts` (the TypeScript mirror of the database ranking formula) also caps such a card's importance at `config.ranking.airworthinessImportance` (1.5, instead of 2 x 1.25 = 2.5); the real database formula would need the same change in a migration (not part of this folder).
5. **SEC 8-K** (`src/sources/sec.ts`): only for about 150 household-name companies (`config.watchCompanies`, by ticker; CIKs resolved from SEC's ticker list, a ticker missing from the list is logged and skipped; without the list the SEC source is skipped for that run). Filings come from the current 8-K Atom feed plus each watched company's filing list (`https://data.sec.gov/submissions/CIK##########.json`, API host list, last `config.secLookbackDays` = 10 days), merged on accession number. It keeps original 8-Ks (not amendments) with at least one watch item (`config.watchItems`: 1.01, 1.03, 1.05, 2.01, 2.02, 2.05, 4.02, 5.02). A filing whose only watch item is 5.02 is skipped as `routine_5.02` unless one sentence of its Item 5.02 text names the CEO, CFO, chair or president together with a change word (resigns, retires, appointed, steps down, successor, ...); director-only changes, committee seats and pay plans are routine. Item 2.05 (layoffs, restructuring) is category markets with the default rank weight. Filings whose items are only 7.01, 8.01 and 9.01 (Reg FD, other events, exhibits) were skipped (since M9c a watch-list filing of that kind goes to the AI relevance gate instead, see "SEC top-500 coverage and Reg FD" below; without a watch map they are still skipped) before any page fetch or AI call and counted as `skipped` (`config.sec8kLowValueItems`). It then fetches the filing index and the primary document. **Earnings press release (EX-99.1):** an Item 2.02 8-K is usually a cover page; the figures sit in the EX-99.1 exhibit. For a filing with an item in `config.secExhibit.items` (2.02) the script also fetches the exhibit named EX-99.1 (then EX-99.01, then EX-99; `config.secExhibit.types`) from the filing index (`src/sec-text.ts`, `findExhibitUrl` in `src/sources/sec.ts`). It is a `www.sec.gov` /Archives/ page, so it goes through the same allowlist, rate limiter and cache as every other page. Its text (capped at `config.sourceTextMaxChars` like the primary document) goes to the AI first, followed by at most `config.secExhibit.primaryMaxChars` (3,000) characters of the primary document. If there is no EX-99.1 or it cannot be fetched, a line is logged and the primary document alone is used. Other exhibits are not followed.
   - **Category and event type come from the item numbers, in code:** 2.02, 4.01, 4.02 → earnings / earnings; 1.01, 1.02, 2.01 → deals / deal; 5.02 → markets / exec_change; 1.05 (cyber incident) → tech / none; 1.03 (bankruptcy) and 3.01 (delisting notice) → markets / none. When several apply, the order is 1.03, 2.02, 4.02, 4.01, 1.05, 2.01, 5.02, 1.01, 1.02, 3.01. Only when no item decides (for example 2.05 or 2.06 alone) is the AI's choice used, limited to the 9 categories and the event types earnings, deal, exec_change, insider.
5b. **Broad topics** (feed or API text only; hosts on the API list, never pages; category and event type fixed in code; the AI's category is ignored):
   - **Wikipedia "In the news"** (`src/sources/wiki.ts`, `--only wiki`) -> World. The featured feed of the last `config.wiki.lookbackDays` (1) UTC days, today first (`wikiFeedUrl(now, daysBack)`; default 1 = today only, because live runs showed past-day feeds carry 0 news; setting 2 or 3 reads earlier days too), from `en.wikipedia.org` through the same allowlisted API fetch and parser. A missing `news` key (a past day may have none) = no items; a day whose request fails is logged (`wiki: feed for <date> failed`) and the other days still run. A story that appears on several days is kept once (same `external_id` = story text + link, or same article URL; the newest day wins); a story with no `<!--Mon DD-->` comment is dated to its feed day. The run logs one line with the stories and new-to-this-run count per day. The AI sees the story sentence plus the first linked article's plain-text extract (max 1,500 chars); thumbnails are never read. Source link = that article; license `cc_by_sa`; card and source attribution `Adapted from Wikipedia ("<title>") · CC BY-SA 4.0`; 10-word copy limit. The request carries a Wikimedia-format User-Agent (`Element/0.1 (<contact>) node-fetch`), the contact taken from `SEC_USER_AGENT`.
   - **CPSC recalls** (`src/sources/cpsc.ts`, `--only cpsc`) -> Recalls & Safety, event `recall`. saferproducts.gov JSON, last 14 days, newest first. **The cap of 5 (`config.cpsc.maxPerRun`) counts only NEW recalls**: every recall in the window is a candidate, `processItems` records each (`api.upsert_item`) and skips the ones an earlier run already handled without counting them, and stops after 5 new ones (`limits.total`; the rest are not even recorded). So each run moves on to the next unprocessed recalls instead of re-picking the 5 newest. A new recall that the relevance gate drops (item closed as `skipped`) still counts as processed; one left `new` (no AI available) is retried and counts again. The AI sees Title, Description, Hazards and Remedies; source link = the recall URL in the JSON; credit "Source: U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission". License partly verified (US government work). **Material verdict** (`classifyRecall`, deterministic, no AI): material when units >= 100,000, units x listed price >= $10M, deaths or serious injuries are reported in the Injuries text (the "risk of serious injury or death" title wording does not count; "No injuries have been reported" sentences are ignored), a stop-sale, or a watch-list recalling firm. After publish the verdict, its reason codes and the company tickers go to `api.set_card_material`; the run log counts `recall_material` / `recall_not_material` and logs `recall material: <reasons>` or `recall not material (Safety topic only): minor`. A failed write leaves the card without a verdict, so it stays out of the feeds (fails closed). Not-material recalls are still published: they are listed under Recalls & Safety, on the company page and in search, never in Trending, Latest or For you (migration `20261116000000_macro_focus.sql`).
   - **USGS significant earthquakes** (`src/sources/usgs.ts`, `--only usgs`) -> Science & Space, event `science`. Template card, no AI: headline = a plain "Magnitude 5.7 earthquake hits Pakistan", body = magnitude, broad place and alert level (and an ocean-region note). Source link = the event page; credit "Credit: U.S. Geological Survey". **Publishing threshold:** yellow, orange and red PAGER alerts always publish. A quake whose alert is green or missing is skipped (`skip low_interest (<reason>)`, counted as skipped) when it is at sea / on an ocean ridge or rise / has no country in its place name (no comma, or a ridge, rise, ocean, sea, trench word: `config.usgs.atSeaPattern`), or when its magnitude is under `config.usgs.minMagnitudeWithoutAlert` (6.5).
   - **NASA news releases** (`src/sources/nasa.ts`, `--only nasa`) -> Science & Space. RSS; only links under `https://www.nasa.gov/news-release/` (APOD, image articles, blog posts and podcasts dropped). **Why only 1 of 10 feed items counted (2026-10-17):** the filter is correct. The feed returns just its 10 newest posts and mixes in APOD and image articles, so a quiet few days leave one news release. The run reads `config.nasa.feedPages` (1) pages (default 1 = page 1 only, because live runs showed `?paged=2` answers HTTP 403; the multi-page code stays: page 2 = the feed URL plus `?paged=2`, same allowlisted host; with feedPages 2 a failing older page is logged and skipped; pages can overlap and are deduplicated by link) and keeps releases from the last `config.nasa.maxAgeDays` (14, was 7) days. The log line `nasa: N feed items over 1 page(s), ...` shows how many pages were read. The AI sees the release text from the feed with figures and captions removed. Credit "Source: NASA".
   - **NewsData.io link-out cards (M9, branch m9c-linkout, OFF)** (`src/sources/newsdata.ts`, `--only newsdata_market,newsdata_latest`). Link-out only (rule 7): the publisher's headline verbatim (200-char cap), the publisher URL, "via NewsData.io" and "Delayed 12 h" (a database trigger sets `delayed_hours` 12); never a description, content, image or AI text (the parser keeps six fields only). Three locks: `config.newsdata.enabled` / env `NEWSDATA_ENABLED=1`, env `NEWSDATA_API_KEY` (no key = no-op), and the database flag `core.app_config.linkout_cards`. `validatePublish` accepts `link_out` only with the flag on and origin `newsdata` (`checkLinkOut`). Each run costs one credit, counted before the request (`core.ai_usage` provider `newsdata`, cap `newsdata.dailyCreditCap` 144 of the free 200). Standing content rule: finance, macro and investing headlines only; recalls and micro-cap news skipped unless a watch-list company is named; advice wording, sports and celebrity skipped; headlines the same story as a live card (60% shared key words) skipped. Cron and delay migrations: `20261201000100_newsdata_cron.sql`, `20261201000000_newsdata_linkout.sql` (written, not applied). Test: `test/newsdata.test.ts`.
   - **Agency data cards (D-113, 2026-11-10)** (`src/agencyRun.ts`, `--only eia,census,auctions,fomc`). Template cards (kind `data`, no AI call, no page download, "No AI used"), each citing the agency by name (rule 8), published through `publishBlurb` with a dedupe key of agency + series + period, so a rerun is a no-op. Terms read per source: `docs/work/sources-eia-census-treasury-fed.md` (local file). **EIA** (`src/sources/eia.ts`, key `EIA_API_KEY`, public domain): weekly petroleum stocks (crude headline, gasoline and distillate in the detail) and weekly natural gas storage; retail gasoline is coded but OFF (`agency.eia.gasoline.enabled`) because FRED `GASREGW` already carries that number. About 2 cards a week, 1 request per report, at most 3 cards per run. **Census** (`src/sources/census.ts`, optional `CENSUS_API_KEY`): retail sales and housing starts, **OFF by default** (`agency.census.enabled` false) until Drew accepts the API notice requirement; cards carry the "not endorsed or certified by the Census Bureau" notice and dedupe per month (R-37); RSAFS and HOUST already come through FRED. **Treasury auctions** (`src/sources/treasuryauctions.ts`, FiscalData `auctions_query`, no key): note and bond results only (bills and TIPS skipped), newest first, at most 2 new cards per run, about 0.5 a day. **FOMC calendar** (`src/sources/fomc.ts`): one card per meeting, 7 days before it starts, from the dated list in `agency.fomc.meetings` (copied from the Fed calendar page; refresh yearly; 2027 dates tentative). FOMC **minutes** need no new code: they arrive in `press_monetary.xml` through `fed.ts` (page host `www.federalreserve.gov`, rule 2, public domain, 25-word copy limit, labelled "FOMC minutes"). The fetch wrapper has no conditional requests (no ETag), so the load is held down by cadence (3 cron jobs, weekdays only) and `length`/`page[size]` limits instead.
   Wikipedia, CPSC and NASA cards need the AI (relevance gate applies); their template cards are not published.
6. For each news item, in order:
   1. Duplicate and burst guard (`src/dedupe.ts`): same URL means skip; at most 3 cards per ticker within 24 hours.
   2. `api.upsert_item` records the item; items handled in an earlier run are skipped. **Per-run caps count only new items** (`NewLimits` in `src/pipeline.ts`): an item an earlier run already handled never uses up a cap, so every run moves on to the next unprocessed item. Used by CPSC (5 new recalls) and the FAA airworthiness cap; the run-wide `--limit` budget was already new-only (it is spent after this check).
   3. Summarizer chain (`src/summarizer/chain.ts`), order set by `config.providerOrder` = Gemini, Groq, template. The license check comes first: only public-domain and SEC filing text may be sent, to any provider.
      - **Gemini** (`src/summarizer/gemini.ts`, JSON output) is tried first. A card it writes must pass the validator; a rejected card is retried once with the validator's errors.
      - **Groq** (`src/summarizer/groq.ts`, OpenAI-compatible chat API, `openai/gpt-oss-120b`, JSON schema output) is used when Gemini's daily cap is reached, on a 429 or other HTTP error, on a timeout, when `GEMINI_API_KEY` is missing, or after both Gemini attempts were rejected. Same prompt, same validator, same one retry. Groq gets at most 6,000 characters of source text because its free tier is limited by tokens per minute.
      - **Template card** (`src/summarizer/template.ts`, no AI, built only from the feed's metadata, plain wording, `ai_generated = false`) if both fail or are capped. Template cards have no relevance score, so they are only published for Fed and BEA releases and for watch-list 8-Ks whose main item is 2.02, 5.02, 1.05, 4.02 or 2.05 (`config.templateSecItems`). Other items stay `new` and are retried on a later run with a working AI provider.
      - **Everyday relevance:** the AI also returns `everyday_relevance` (1 to 5, prompt `sum-v7`: how much does the item matter to a reader who follows business, the economy, markets and investing; 5 = moves markets or the economy, 3 = useful context and the default for an ordinary product recall, 2 = minor or science news with no economic angle, 1 = paperwork; the rubric is `RELEVANCE_RUBRIC` in `src/summarizer/gemini.ts`). Below `config.minEverydayRelevance` (3) the card is not published; the log says `skip low_relevance (n)` and the score is stored in `ingest.items.status_note`. The skip costs only that one AI call (no retry). For a published AI card the score is also stored on the card (`core.blurbs.everyday_relevance`, migration `20261123000000_everyday_relevance.sql`, written by `api.publish_blurb` and `api.revise_blurb`); it is not part of the card JSON. Ranking can use it later to keep relevance-3 recalls out of Trending, Latest and For you. Template and data cards carry no score (null).
      - **In-depth detail (prompt `sum-v3`):** the same AI call also writes `detail` (shown when a card is opened): 2-4 short paragraphs (at most 180 words together) and 3-5 key points (at most 15 words each). It is checked like the card (numbers in the source, no advice words, copy limit per license). If only the detail fails, the card is published with `detail` null. Template and data cards get a factual detail built from their own fields (`src/detail.ts`; data cards: value, change, previous value for rates, what the number measures, the agency).
      - **Writing style (prompt `sum-v2`, kept in `sum-v3` to `sum-v5`):** plain words at about an 8th-grade reading level, short sentences, no jargon (unavoidable terms explained in a few words); sentence 1 says what happened; the last sentence says why it matters to a regular person only if that follows from the source.
      - **Prompt `sum-v5` (2026-10-18):** (1) headlines in sentence case (capitalise only the first word and proper nouns); (2) never model numbers, SKUs, UPCs or lot numbers in the headline or body, key points only (the validator also rejects the outright words "SKU", "UPC", "model number", "lot number" there); (3) an earnings card must state the real figures from the source and never describe the filing itself (exhibits, press release, conference call, links). Template version `tpl-v5`: sentence-case Fed/BEA template headlines (`src/text.ts` `sentenceCase`, which only lowercases a list of everyday words, so names, tickers and acronyms keep their capitals) and a generic CPSC template (no title, so no product codes). The version bump makes `--rewrite` revisit every card written with an older version.
      - **Prompt `sum-v6` (2026-10-18, flow and no repetition, D-58):** the headline and the summary read as one piece. Body sentence 1 continues from the headline and adds the next most useful fact (it never restates the headline); every sentence adds something new; no repeated phrases; no filler ("This means that...", "In summary..."); the detail key points do not copy the summary sentences. The prompt (shared by Gemini and Groq, `buildPrompt` in `src/summarizer/gemini.ts`) carries one bad and one good example. The validator enforces it for AI drafts (`checkRepetition` in `src/validate.ts`, thresholds in `config.repetition`): the draft is rejected when (a) the first summary sentence starts with the headline, or at least 70% of the headline's content words (stop words, numbers and words under 3 letters ignored, light stemming) appear in it, or (b) two summary sentences are near-duplicates (70% of the shorter one's content words are in the other). A rejection goes through the usual retry once with the reason, then the template fallback. Template and data cards are exempt (the chain sets `checkFlow` only for AI drafts): their fixed wording, such as "X reports a change in its top leaders" / "X told the SEC about a change among its top leaders", would fail (a).
      - **Prompt `sum-v7` (2026-11-10):** readers are "busy everyday people interested in business, finance, stocks and investing (about 16 to 50)" at about an 8th-grade reading level (the college-student wording is gone). The relevance rubric scores market and macro significance (see above). Rules added to the prompt: a short list of terms that need no explanation, any other technical term defined in parentheses the first time, plain-word swaps ("cash burn", "from the previous quarter", "from a year earlier", "percentage points", "forecast"), one direction word per change ("fell 18% to"), claims and aims attributed to who made them, the parties the source names, no guessing and no relative time, dates as numerals, acronyms and proper nouns in capitals, basis points kept when the source says basis points, the first sentence adds the most repeatable fact, the last sentence gives a "why it matters" only when the source states it, and an earnings block (prior-period comparison, launch dates, named investors). The recall block must name the danger and scores an ordinary recall 3. The prompt is trimmed: on the same source text, live Gemini calls measured +515 prompt tokens over sum-v6 (earnings 4,239 to 4,754; recall 1,914 to 2,429); the offline estimate (`npm run prompt-tokens`, tested to stay under +600) shows +310 to +392 by characters and +429 to +544 with the conservative ratio. The rubric lives in the prompt only (the JSON schemas point to it). Source text sits between `<<<SOURCE TEXT START>>>` and `<<<SOURCE TEXT END>>>`; text inside is data (a line telling the model to change a score is ignored).
      - **sum-v7 checks (AI drafts only, `src/styleChecks.ts`, called from `validateDraft`, `validateDetail` and `validateBriefSummary`):** `style:` (dates or years spelled out, field labels and record dumps such as "From:" or "Filed with the SEC on" at a sentence start, "Label: value" key points, a doubled direction word such as "fell 18% down to", a headline that starts with a bare verb or ends in "...", "pts"), `speculation:` (`config.speculationWords`: could, might, likely, is expected to, is set to, poised to, lower-case may) and `relative_time:` (`config.relativeTimeWords`: today, yesterday, tomorrow, this/next/last week, this/next/last month, days away). The last two fire only when the source text does not use the word itself. Also: `event_type` must be one of `config.aiEventTypes` (earnings, exec_change, deal, insider, macro_release, fed) or null (enum in both JSON schemas); the repetition check also covers detail paragraph 1 and key point 1 against the headline, and a key point that repeats the headline's first number without at least two new content words (`checkDetailRepetition`, errors start with "detail repetition:", limit `config.repetition.detailHeadlineOverlapMax`, default 0.7 like the card; a detail that fails any check is dropped and the card is published without it); number scale tolerance (`missingNumbers`, `config.numberScaleTolerance`): "1.5 billion" is accepted when the source says "1,500 million" (one scale step, only when the draft figure has a scale word, never for percentages, never rounded); the advice phrase "to the moon" counts only within 60 characters of a market word (`config.adviceMarketContext`), so "missions to the Moon" no longer rejects NASA cards. Template and data cards are exempt from the first three.
      - **Brief summary (`src/briefSummary.ts`):** the brief prompt follows the same readers, style and casing rules, puts the Federal Reserve and the biggest economic numbers (GDP, payrolls, CPI, unemployment) in paragraph 1 before any single company, joins no topics with "Meanwhile" or "In other news", asks for definitions in parentheses (square brackets fail the plain-text rule), and says "the period this brief covers" instead of "today" (the daily window is 24, 48 or 72 hours). `validateBriefSummary` adds the style, speculation and relative-time checks ("today" and "this week" are allowed there as the brief's own span). Topic sections in `config.mail.summarySkipSectionCategories` (safety and science) are left out of the text the AI sees, so a recall or a NASA item is summarized only when it is a TOP STORY.
      - **Casing fixer (`fixProperNouns` in `src/text.ts`, lists in `config.properNounFixes`):** after validation, an accepted AI draft (headline, body, detail, and the brief summary in `cleanSummaryText`) is fixed for "Nasa" to "NASA", "Fcc", "Faa", "Fomc", "united states", "federal reserve", "u.s." and lower-case "mars" or "moon" next to a space word. Text that is already right comes back unchanged (tested on the 42 contract fixture cards), and a second run changes nothing.
      - **`--rewrite` hide cap:** the new rubric re-gates old cards. `runRewrite` hides a card for low relevance only while hidden / selected stays within `config.rewriteHideMaxFraction` (0.4); past that the card is kept and the log says `kept (hide cap 0.4 reached ...)`. A rewrite also stores the new score (`everyday_relevance` in the revise payload). Start with `--limit 20` and read the log.

      - Each AI provider has its own daily cap and its own spacing between calls (Gemini 500/day, 5 s; Groq 62/day, 30.1 s: `groq.estimatedTokensPerCall` 3,215 = the sum-v6 estimate 2,700 + the 515 tokens sum-v7 adds, so the cap is floor(200,000 / 3,215) = 62, was 74; the live copy is the `core.ai_caps` row, which keeps a reserve of 5 for the brief). Spacing is `60 s / (RPM x rpmSafetyFactor)` with a 0.8 factor, so Gemini runs at 12 calls a minute, not its full 15. After a 429 the provider is skipped for 60 s for the rest of the run (`rateLimitCooldownMs`). If a draft fails only because the body is too long and the headline fits, the body is cut after the last whole sentence that fits and checked again; a headline that is too long is never cut. The card records the model that actually wrote it. A provider without a key is skipped (`src/summarizer/providers.ts`); the start-up line lists which ones are active.
   4. The validator (`src/validate.ts`) checks:
      - shape and length (headline 90 characters, body 60 words)
      - no advice language
      - every number and ticker must appear in the source text
      - the copy limit: at most 10 consecutive words from a filing, 25 from public-domain government text
      - **no_figures:** a card whose event type is earnings (in code, from the 8-K items) must have at least one digit in its body, or it is rejected with reason `no_figures` (retry with the error, then the next provider, then the template). For SEC filings it applies to filings with Item 2.02 (`config.earningsFigures.requiredItems`); a card from 4.01 (auditor change) or 4.02 (restatement notice) alone is exempt. The template fallback is held to the same rule: a figure-less earnings template card is skipped (`skip no_figures`; the item is closed as `skipped: no_figures` when an AI draft was tried, and stays `new` when no AI was available so a later run retries). A figure-less template earnings card is therefore never published.
      - no model numbers, SKUs, UPCs or lot numbers by name in the headline or body (`product codes in headline or body`)
      - sum-v7 (AI drafts): style, speculation, relative time, the `event_type` enum, repetition in the detail, number scale tolerance (see "sum-v7 checks" above)
   5. `api.publish_blurb` stores the card and its source link, then ranks it. The database repeats the duplicate, ticker-cap, source and license checks.
7. A summary line per card and a final count are printed.

**Hardening (watchdog, 2026-11-08).** Every request ends after `config.fetchTimeoutMs` (20 s) including the body read (`fetchAllowed`, `timeoutMs` option, `TimeoutError`); the Gemini and Groq adapters pass it down, and an HTTP 404 (or a 400 saying the model was not found) switches the same call to `config.gemini.fallbackModel` / `config.groq.alternativeModel` and sets `modelMissing` on the provider. ## Card supply operations (M9 workstream 1)

Why: the 30-day median is 5 cards a day, about 316 items were marked skipped by `report_card_revise_failed`, and Groq reaches its daily cap while Gemini is barely used (readout of 2026-11-10). The target is twice the median (about 10 cards a day); the floor per market hour is set after the readout is rerun.

**Measure it (read-only).** `node scripts/supply-readout.mjs` runs the queries in `supabase/tests/supply_readout.sql` one at a time through `npx supabase db query --remote --read-only` (your own Supabase CLI login; no key is read or stored). It prints cards per ET day by source and by category (30 days), the daily median and the 2x target, market hours with zero cards (14 days), cards by hour of day, live cards per topic, items by source, status and reason, AI calls against the caps, the Gemini / Groq / template share, and the pool size. `--only items_by_status,pool_size` runs some of them; `--print-sql` prints the SQL to paste into the Supabase SQL editor; `--json` prints JSON. Each query is a single `select`, and the script refuses anything else. Indexes: the cards-per-time queries use `blurbs_published_at_idx` and the items query uses `items_fetched_at_idx` (both are added, not yet applied, in `supabase/migrations/20261129000000_supply_ops.sql`; without them the queries scan tables of a few thousand rows).

**Report-card failures are retryable.** Before, an item the report-card step rejected (`report_card_revise_failed`: the one rewrite failed its checks; `report_card_abandon`: the scorer said the card should not run) was marked `skipped` and never looked at again. Now `src/retry.ts` keeps it `new` with a counter in `ingest.items.status_note` (`report_card_retry:<n>:<code>`, one short string that is overwritten, so it cannot grow). Each later run that sees the item in its source feed tries again, and the third failure (`config.reportCard.maxRetries`) skips it for good with the plain code. A scorer failure (rate limit, invalid JSON) still publishes the card unscored, as before, and `duplicate_story` is final at once. The protocol itself stays off by default (`REPORT_CARD_PROTOCOL`, `config.reportCard.protocolEnabled`). `api.upsert_item` returns `status_note` for this (same migration). Retries cost writer calls (up to 2 more per item) and stay inside the daily caps. Tests: `test/retry.test.ts`, `test/protocol.test.ts`.

**Requeue what was already skipped.** `scripts/requeue-skipped.sql` is applied by hand, never by the pipeline. Step 1 is a read-only dry run that counts the candidates by reason. Step 2 is commented out, so a dry run cannot write; it sets items back to `new` when they are `skipped` with a `report_card_%` note, were fetched within 14 days and have no card, at most 100 rows per run. Repeat step 2 until it returns 0 rows. A requeued item is only picked up while its source still lists it (the Fed feed reaches back 7 days, CPSC and NASA 14); the rest stay `new` and do nothing. Review the dry run before applying.

**Groq cap from measured tokens.** `groqCapFromMeasuredTokens(measuredTokensPerCall)` in `src/config.ts` returns `min(freeRpd, floor(freeTpd x 0.9 / measuredTokensPerCall))`, or 0 when there is no measurement. With the free limits of 200,000 tokens a day and 1,000 requests a day, 2,700 tokens a call gives 74 without the margin, and 3,200 tokens a call gives 56 with it. Use it after the sum-v7 prompt is measured (its prompt is longer). It is a calculator that nothing calls: `groqDailyCap()` and the caps (Gemini 500, Groq 74) are unchanged.

**Cadence design (documented only, nothing built).**
- *Minimum per market hour.* Market hours are Mon-Fri 09:00-15:59 ET. The watchdog already reads `cards_1h` (cards published in the last 60 minutes) in the same `ops_status` call. The setting `supply_floor_cards_market_hour` (Worker var `SUPPLY_FLOOR_CARDS_MARKET_HOUR`) is 0, which means off. After the readout is rerun with the retry fix, count the share of market hours in the last 14 days that had at least N cards (query `market_hour_gaps_14d`), pick the largest N that 90 percent of hours reach, and start at 1. The daily floor (`supply_floor_cards_24h`, 10) alerts on its own. Both alerts only report; they never publish anything.
- *Meeting the floor without lowering quality.* In this order: (1) the retry and requeue above, which recovers items already paid for; (2) spread publishing, so cards from one source do not all land in one run (the per-source soft cap in `config.selection`); (3) public-domain agency sources, each with its terms read first; (4) a quiet hour is allowed, and a card must never be made just to fill an hour.
- *7-day backfill at release.* The stories of the last 7 days that the feeds still list are published once, so the feed is full on release day. Steps: (1) apply the migration and, if the dry run looks right, `requeue-skipped.sql` one 100-row batch at a time; (2) deploy the functions; (3) run `node src/main.ts --once --only <source>` for each source with a higher `--limit` than the hourly run (a laptop run with the usual caps; stop when Groq reaches its cap); (4) `select core.refresh_all_ranks();`. A backfilled card keeps its original publication time in `sort_at` and `as_of`, so Latest orders it by when the news happened (check this on the dev project first). `published_at` is the time it was added, so the 24-hour supply count rises for a day: read the watchdog floor and the readout with that in mind. The macro history backfill (`--backfill-macro`) only fills the Numbers charts and never makes cards. Nothing here runs by itself; each step is started by hand.

## Yield fixes (M9c, R-300)

Found in the 2026-11-11 readout (not in the R-254 table): the cloud `sec` run takes about 104 s of its 100 s soft budget and ends with "stopped early (time): 180 items left", and ONE item (a Best Buy 2.02 from Aug 27) used 2 Gemini and 2 Groq calls every hour for weeks. Cause: an item whose AI drafts were rejected and that could not be a template card ended the run with its status still `new`, so every hourly run retried it (96 calls a day, the whole Groq cap of 74). What changed:
- **Bulk handled check.** `publisher.handledUrls(urls)` (`api.items_handled`, migration `20261133000000_items_handled.sql`, service role only) returns the URLs an earlier run finished. `dropHandled` in `src/pipeline.ts` calls it once per source and removes them before the per-item loop, so a run no longer pays one `api.upsert_item` round trip (about 0.2 s) for each of about 340 filings it has already handled. A missing method or a failing call changes nothing (the per-item check still runs).
- **Bounded retry.** `src/retry.ts` `decideSoftRetry`: when AI drafts were rejected and the item cannot become a card (`template_not_allowed`, or `no_figures` without a rejected-for-figures draft), it stays `new` with `status_note = retry:<n>:<code>` and is skipped on the third failure (`config.softRetry.maxFailures`). A run where no AI answered (cap, rate limit) does not count. The skip log lines now end with the chain log (provider outcomes and validator error names, no source text).
- **Gemini overload.** HTTP 500 to 504 from Gemini now tries the other Gemini model once (`config.gemini.fallbackModel`, its own capacity and its own free quota) before the chain falls to Groq; the extra request counts against the daily cap. Until now a 503 sent the item to Groq (30 s pacing, 62 calls a day).
- **Template cards for 1.01 and 2.01.** `config.templateSecItems` gains `1.01` (material agreement) and `2.01` (completed acquisition or sale), so a watch-list filing the AI could not write still becomes a short card that names the SEC and links the filing (plain wording in `src/summarizer/template.ts`, rules 1 to 10 unchanged). Earnings (2.02) templates stay unpublished because they carry no figures.
- **Work cap and window.** `config.workCapBySource.sec = 10` (default 5, request `work_cap` still wins, maximum 20), and a routine 5.02 skip gives its slot back (`guardPublisher`). `config.secLookbackDays` 90 to 21: fewer stale candidates, no quarter-old cards.
- **Readout.** `supply_readout.sql` has a `stuck_new_items` query (should be 0 for new rows after deploy).
- Not changed on purpose: the relevance gate (`minEverydayRelevance` 3), recalls (kept out of the main feeds by the standing content rule; the 139 published and 118 report-card-failed CPSC items are mostly the laptop backfill), Federal Register (20 documents in 90 days, all handled: supply, not a leak), Reg FD 7.01 and 8.01 filings (126 per run are skipped before any AI call; opening them with the relevance gate is the next lever if more cards are needed, about 10 to 20 more AI calls a day).

## SEC top-500 coverage and Reg FD (M9c, R-360)

Until now `runSec` made one data.sec.gov request per company (about 190) and the Edge Function wall limit is about 140 s, so the list could not grow. Now (branch m9c-sec2, nothing applied or deployed):

- **Watch list: about 540 companies.** `config.watchCompanies` (188 household names, unchanged) plus `config.secExtraWatch` (about 350 S&P 500 style large caps from `contract/companiesExtra.ts`, generated by `scripts/gen-companies-extra.mjs` from `scripts/sec-extra-list.json` and SEC's own `company_tickers_exchange.json`; the SEC rows are kept in `src/fixtures/sec-company-tickers-extra.json`). CIKs are resolved from SEC's ticker list at run time as before; a ticker SEC does not list is logged and skipped. Same 8-K rules for both lists (`watchItems`, routine 5.02 filter, templates only for `templateSecItems`).
- **Finding filings without 540 requests (`config.secIndex`).** (1) The EDGAR daily index `https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/daily-index/<yyyy>/QTR<q>/form.<yyyymmdd>.idx` (www.sec.gov, a page host; about 1 MB a day, no key) for the last 3 calendar days: `parseDailyIndex` keeps plain 8-K lines, the watched CIKs are picked out, the filing index URLs go to `publisher.handledUrls` (one `api.items_handled` call) and only CIKs with a filing the database has not handled get one data.sec.gov submissions request (that is where the item numbers are), at most `maxNewCiksPerRun` (60). (2) A rotating safety slice of `rotationPerRun` (30) watched CIKs from the clock (UTC hour count modulo the number of slices, `rotationSlice`), so every company's own list is re-read about every 18 hours even if the index is down. (3) The current 8-K Atom feed, now `count=100` (was 40). Typical run: tickers file, feed, 3 index files and 30 to 90 submissions requests, under the shared 5 requests a second limiter (SEC allows 10; descriptive `SEC_USER_AGENT` as before). Log lines: `sec: daily index N days read, X watched 8-Ks, Y not handled yet, Z companies to read` and `sec: reading N company filing lists (...)`.
- **Reg FD (7.01) and other events (8.01).** A watch-list filing whose card items are only 7.01/8.01 (9.01 exhibits do not count) is no longer dropped as `low_value`; it goes to the AI relevance gate (`minEverydayRelevance` 3, unchanged). Cost control (`config.secRegFd`): at most `maxPerRun` (1) such filing per run is worked on (`skip regfd_cap` for the rest; they are not recorded, so a later run can take them); `isBoilerplateRegFd` drops pure boilerplate after the page text is read and before any AI call (`skip boilerplate_regfd`, closed as skipped): fewer than `minWords` (14) words left after removing legal "furnished, not filed" and forward-looking sentences, exhibit pointers and signatures (unless a dollar or percent figure is in what is left), conference or webcast attendance, or a slide deck, with no figure. - Tests: `test/sec-coverage-m9c.test.ts` (index parser and URLs, rotation covers every company once per cycle, extra list valid and in sync, Reg FD gate and boilerplate cases, end-to-end runSec with fake fetches). ## Known limits

- Feeds are parsed with regular expressions sized for these sources. A layout change at the SEC or Fed can break parsing; tests and the dry run show it.
- Most 8-K primary documents are thin cover pages, so many SEC cards will be template cards. That is intended: they stay truthful.
- The SEC ticker-list layout and the Gemini and Groq request/response fields follow the official docs but were not checked against a live response. If Groq rejects the JSON schema mode with HTTP 400, the adapter retries that call once in plain JSON mode.
- Groq pacing (30 s between calls) means a run where Gemini is down entirely takes about 10 minutes for 20 items.

## Per-run state

`runOnce` keeps its mutable state (topic counters and narrow-source caps, recent-card list, selection queue) in an `AsyncLocalStorage`, so two runs in one process (a warm Edge Function isolate) never share it (`test/concurrent-runs.test.ts`).
